Why your inflation rate may differ from the official rate

The official inflation rate published by governments is an average based on a "basket" of goods and services representing the spending of a typical household. But no household is actually average.

Your personal inflation rate depends on how your spending is distributed. If you spend a large share of your budget on categories where prices are rising quickly — for example food, transport, or electricity — your inflation rate may be significantly higher than the official figure.

Conversely, if most of your spending is in categories where prices are stable or rising slowly, your personal inflation rate may be lower.

What this calculator shows

This calculator estimates your personal inflation rate by weighting the inflation of different spending categories according to how much of your budget they represent.

It is a simplified model, but it illustrates an important point: inflation is not experienced equally. The same national inflation rate can translate into very different cost-of-living changes for different households.

Better questions lead to clearer thinking. Instead of asking only “what is THE inflation rate?”, it is often more useful to ask: “what is MY inflation rate?”
